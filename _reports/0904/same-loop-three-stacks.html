Same Loop, Three Stacks

All three run the same eight-slot loop. What differs is which slots were promoted into their own system, whether generation and training share GPUs, and therefore how weights move between them.

8 slots in every stack 1 GPU · 8 GPUs · 20 nodes colocated vs disjoint is the fork

The one sentence

Nobody replaces the algorithm — they replace the plumbing around it. Group advantages are five lines of tensor maths in all three. The difference is that at 0.6B one process does everything, at 14B two systems take turns on the same GPUs, and at 397B two systems run on separate hardware and must be kept in sync while both stay busy.

2 · The loop, annotated three ways

The left column is your code, line for line. Each row shows what fills that slot in each stack. The colour says how much machinery the slot costs, not how important it is.

A natural follow-up: where does Hugging Face sit in any of this? All three use a Qwen3 checkpoint, so it is tempting to assume HF plays the same role everywhere. It does not — and "HF" is really five separable products, each adopted or declined independently.

The short answer to "does HF's job end once the model is loaded?" In ch07, yes — HF is a CDN and a tokenizer library, nothing more. In the SWE stack it supplies the module definition, which phitrain then rewrites in place — see the next section. At 397B the HF module is never instantiated at all: the weights are converted and Megatron's own layer classes run. TRL is used by none of them — grep -rl "from trl" phitrain/ is empty.

3 · How the checkpoint becomes a running model

All three start from Qwen3 weights, so "we load an HF checkpoint" sounds like the same sentence three times. It is not. The question that separates them is whether the Hugging Face nn.Module ever exists inside the training process at all.

What "rewritten in place" actually means

The mutation is not a wrapper around the model — it reaches into the decoder stack and replaces what each layer is and where its parameters live.

Why phitrain can do this and Megatron cannot. Rewriting in place only works if the module follows a predictable shape — a list of homogeneous decoder layers at a known attribute. That holds for dense models like Qwen3-14B, so FSDP2 sharding plus optional TP is enough. Megatron targets models where parallelism must be designed into the layer itself (pipeline stages, expert routing across ranks), which cannot be retrofitted onto a module written without it. That is the real reason 397B converts and 14B does not.

Answering the question directly: for SWE, Ray + phitrain's RayTuner replaces SkyRL, and PyTorch FSDP replaces Megatron. There is no Megatron anywhere in phitrain — a 14B dense model with CPU offload fits under plain FSDP sharding, so none of tensor, pipeline, expert or context parallelism is needed. vLLM is the one component all three stacks share.

5 · What a "rollout" actually is

This is the largest difference between the three, and it is not a scaling difference — it is a difference in what the task is.

6 · Who owns what — the phitrain / phiagent split

The SWE stack is two in-house layers over open-source parts. The split is by concern, not by scale:

phiagent — the task side

What a rollout is. Agents (mini_swe, ToolCallingAgent), tool-call codecs (hermes vs xml), environment providers (R2E-Gym, SWE-bench Docker), verifiers, message formatters. Knows nothing about GPUs or gradients.

phitrain — the training side

What happens to the trajectories. Ray tuners (grpo, ppo, cispo, isft, opd), FSDP actors, the rollout pool, weight sync, datasets, checkpointing. Knows nothing about bash or patches.

Ray — the substrate

Process placement, actor lifecycle, cross-worker RPC. RayTuner is where the loop lives; ray_worker.py is what runs on each GPU. This is the layer SkyRL occupies in the frontier stack.

The recipe — the glue

recipes/rl/tool_agent/ is a thin binding: it registers the R2E-Gym env provider and PatchTestReward, then hands a config to get_tuner. The YAML you read is the experiment.

The seam to notice: phiagent and phitrain meet only at the Task — an agent, a reward, a verifier, a dataset. Everything above that line is "what does a good answer look like"; everything below is "how do I get gradients onto 8 GPUs". SkyRL draws the same line and calls the upper half an agent harness.

phitrain also ships BroadcastWeightSynchronizer, which is the disjoint mechanism — the same idea as the frontier stack. The SWE config simply does not need it at 8 GPUs.

The three examples sit at three rungs of that ladder, and each adds exactly the machinery its constraint demands — no more. ch07 needs none of it. SWE needs vLLM, FSDP and an agent harness, but not model parallelism and not async. The 397B run needs everything.
