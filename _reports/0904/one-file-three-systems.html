One File, Three Systems

The same seven steps run in both. What changes at scale is not the algorithm — it is that generation, training and orchestration stop being able to share one process, and every seam between them becomes a system you have to operate.

The one sentence

Today the generator and the trainer are the same object in memory. Every tool in the frontier stack — vLLM, Megatron, SkyRL — exists because at scale they cannot be, and once they are separate processes with separate shardings, someone has to move weights between them, keep them numerically identical, and keep both busy.

5 · What each tool is actually for

vLLM — the generator

Replaces step 2 only. Paged attention lets hundreds of sequences share KV cache without pre-allocating worst-case memory; continuous batching keeps the GPU saturated as sequences finish at different times. You reach for it when generation dominates wall-clock — which it already does for you.

Megatron — the trainer

Replaces steps 5-7. Splits one model across GPUs four ways: tensor (within a layer), pipeline (across layers), expert (across MoE experts), context (across sequence length). You reach for it when weights + gradients + optimizer states stop fitting.

SkyRL — the loop

Replaces the for statement, which sounds trivial and is not. It owns async scheduling, the weight sync, and the agent harness interface, so generation and training overlap instead of alternating.

What none of them change

Steps 1, 3 and 4 — sampling a prompt, scoring it, normalising rewards within the group. The GRPO idea in your file is the GRPO idea at 397B. The rest is logistics.

The honest reading for ch07. At 0.6B on one H100, the single file is not a simplification for teaching — it is the correct engineering choice. Every component of the frontier stack exists to solve a problem you do not have. The one exception is generation speed: vLLM would pay for itself today, and it is the only piece of that stack that would.
